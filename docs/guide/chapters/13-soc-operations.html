SOC Operations

SOC Ops is where analysts work incidents: read the ranked queue, investigate an incident's evidence, generate the Forge verdict, shape and approve the response plan, and close the incident with a disposition that records what it was (a true or false positive also teaches Forge's calibration). Every case action here is recorded on the incident's trail under your identity; a verdict run is recorded on the engine's audit chain instead.

How an incident is worked

  1. Queue. SOC Ops lists open incidents ranked by what needs a person first. Each card shows the engine's credibility and, on a node with a calibration, how likely the incident is to be real; otherwise it reads Uncalibrated. The channels All, Urgent Review and Threat Inspection narrow the list.
  2. Investigate. Select an incident. The lineage graph shows how the evidence connects (Material path, Show evidence, Full story), and the investigation dock holds Evidence, Timeline, Model Reasoning, Raw Telemetry, Audit Trail and Notes.
  3. Generate the verdict. The Forge verdict panel runs the narrative and business-impact pipelines on the node's model. It takes minutes, not seconds.
  4. Review the response plan. The engine proposes a plan within the incident's response tier ().
  5. Modify or approve it. Approval is recorded; with enforcement off, each containment step is recorded as refused.
  6. Close the incident with a disposition, which records what the incident was, or without a verdict.

The page header shows Detection active (or disabled) and Enforcement off. The KPI strip shows events analyzed, noise collapsed, material incidents, auto-contained (always zero while enforcement is off), decisions waiting, and how many detection rules are evaluable.

Restrictions

  • A closed incident refuses assign, acknowledge and close; it still accepts notes and a corrected disposition.
  • A note is at most 4,000 characters; text with many accented or non-Latin characters can be refused sooner.
  • A later disposition replaces the earlier one as the incident's verdict, and its calibration effect replaces the earlier one's; the Audit Trail keeps both entries.
  • The Acceptance lapses on date of a risk-accepted verdict can be at most 365 days away; the date picker stops there, and Record disposition stays unavailable for a later date.

Generate verdict and Save plan act without a confirmation step. After a verdict run starts, the panels do not refresh on their own: select the incident again after a few minutes to see the narrative.

If a verdict run is refused, ForgeCentral may say The run request did not reach the engine. even though the engine answered. Running it again is safe: the engine does not start a second run while one is recorded or running.

Generate a verdict

Procedure
  1. Choose SOC Ops and select an incident.
  2. In the Forge verdict panel, choose Generate verdict. The note reads The run is in flight. These panels re-read as the records land. If every record already exists, it reads Every record for this evidence already exists; nothing was re-generated.
  3. After a few minutes, select the incident again. The verdict header shows Generated, the model that wrote it, and the evidence it was generated from.

The model that writes verdicts is chosen on Settings>Configuration (Narrative model).

Modify or approve the response plan

Procedure
  1. Select the incident. Under Coordinated response, read the proposed steps. No response plan has been proposed means there is nothing to approve yet.
  2. To change the plan, choose Modify plan. For each step, edit its title and choose its Action: Investigate (no containment), quarantine or deny. Use Add step and Remove as needed, then choose Save plan. Saving replaces the steps and raises the plan's revision, so an approval of the old plan is refused.
  3. To approve, choose Approve full response and confirm Approve the full response? with Approve. Approved and recorded. Nothing was carried out: enforcement is off on this deployment, so each containment step is recorded refused with its reason.

If approval is refused with The plan changed, was already approved, or does not exist yet, select the incident again to read the current plan.

Assign, acknowledge, note

Procedure
  1. Assign: find the assignee's principal id in the ID column of Users>All Users. In the incident's Case section, enter it in Assign to (principal id), choose Assign, and confirm with Record.
  2. Acknowledge: choose Acknowledge and confirm with Record. It records that a person has seen the incident and changes nothing else.
  3. Note: in the dock, open Notes, type in Case note, choose Record note and confirm. Notes cannot be deleted.

A principal id has the form 00000000-0000-0000-0000-000000000000. The engine records the id you enter as submitted.

Record a disposition

A disposition closes the incident and records what it was. The three true-positive verdicts and False positive teach Forge's calibration; the others do not. False positive also lowers the weight of every incident in the tenant that shares the incident's ATT&CK technique, whichever rule fired.

VerdictRequired fieldEffect
False positiveJustificationCloses the incident, teaches calibration, and lowers the weight of the tenant's incidents with the same technique.
Benign, authorizedAuthorized byCloses the incident; no calibration effect.
True positive, remediatedAction taken: reimage, credential_rotation, patch, isolate or removeCloses the incident and teaches calibration.
True positive, blockedBlocking controlCloses the incident and teaches calibration.

DuplicatePredecessor incident idCloses the incident; no calibration effect.
UndeterminedNoneCloses the incident; the trail records that you ruled it undetermined.
Procedure
  1. In the incident's Case section, under Record a disposition, choose the Verdict (it starts at Undetermined) and fill in its field.
  2. Choose Record disposition, read the confirmation, and choose Record. Verdict recorded: the verdict. The incident is closed. On an incident already closed, the disposition is recorded as a correction.

Choose False positive only when the detection was wrong: it lowers the weight of every incident in the tenant with the same ATT&CK technique until a later disposition of that incident replaces it.

Close without a verdict

Choose Close without verdict and confirm. The incident leaves the queue with no verdict, so calibration learns nothing from it; prefer a disposition when you know what the incident was.

Verify

  • The incident's Audit Trail lists each action with who took it and when, including plan steps the response tier withheld and SIEM write-back outcomes.
  • A closed incident leaves the queue; its disposition appears on the trail.
