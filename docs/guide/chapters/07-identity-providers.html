Identity Providers

An identity-provider connector keeps the Forge user directory in step with your organization's directory. Forge pulls users and groups from the provider on a schedule, so the Users surface, the entity drawer and SOC investigations can say who an account really belongs to. The connector panel appears in two places with the same controls: Users>External IDAM and Settings>Federation.

How identities reach Forge

The Forge user directory brings together three sources: the accounts your identity provider holds, the local accounts and sessions the Forge endpoint agents report from each host, and records you add yourself in ForgeCentral.

  1. Identity provider. The connector polls your Auth0 tenant for changes every delta poll interval and runs a full directory sync on a longer cadence. This chapter configures it.
  2. Endpoint agents. Each Forge endpoint agent reports its host's local accounts, groups and sessions. The engine accepts them within the limits set by the LUG exposure section ().
  3. ForgeCentral. You add and manage local user records on the Users surface ().

There is one connector per Forge node. Every tenant on the node sees the same connector, and users it syncs are placed in the tenant named when the Forge engine was installed, whichever operator configured the connector.

What Save connector does

Save connector runs three steps in order and stops at the first that fails:

  1. It stores the client secret in the node's protected secret store. The secret is not sent over ForgeCentral's connection to the engine (the engine reads it from the store), and ForgeCentral never shows it again.
  2. It points the connector at your Auth0 domain, client ID and audience. The engine starts a new connector with the new settings before it stops the old one, so a mistake does not leave you without a connector.
  3. It enables the connector and sets its two cadences.

Restrictions

  • Auth0 is the only provider in this release.
  • Delta poll interval: 60 to 86,400 seconds (default 300). Full directory sync: 1 to 168 hours (default 24).
  • The audience is optional; when empty, the engine uses the Auth0 Management API audience for your domain.

Connector changes are held in the Forge engine's memory and are not recorded in the audit trail. When the engine restarts, the connector returns to the settings it was installed with. After an engine restart, open the connector and save your settings again.

Configure pre-fills only the domain. You must re-enter the client ID and the client secret, and the cadences reset to 300 seconds and 24 hours; re-enter your own values before saving.

If Save connector fails partway, the earlier steps may already have taken effect (for example, the stored secret may already be replaced). Run Save connector again with every value.

Onboard Auth0

Before you begin
  • In your Auth0 tenant, create (or choose) a machine-to-machine application that can read users and groups through the Auth0 Management API. Note its domain, client ID and client secret.
  • Decide the delta poll interval and the full-sync cadence.
Procedure
  1. Choose Users>External IDAM (or Settings>Federation). With no connector yet, the panel reads No IdAM connector configured.
  2. Choose Onboard Auth0.
  3. Fill in the form ().
  4. Choose Save connector. The button reads Configuring... while the three steps run; on success the form closes and the connector's card appears.
  5. On the card, choose Sync Now to run the first full sync without waiting for the cadence.

Change a connector

Procedure
  1. On the connector's card, choose Configure.
  2. Check the domain, and enter the client ID and the client secret again.
  3. Enter your cadences (the form resets them to 300 seconds and 24 hours).
  4. Choose Save connector.

Run a sync now

Procedure
  1. On the connector's card, choose Sync Now. The button reads Syncing...; the card refreshes every three seconds while the sync runs.
  2. When the sync finishes, read Last sync and Objects synced, and check the state.

Sync Now asks the connector to run a full sync on its next cycle; it does not wait for the result.
