Navigating ForgeCentral

ForgeCentral aims to put every configuration within three clicks of the Overview: choose a destination on the navigation rail, open a tab or a record, then act. A few tasks take more today, for example isolating an entity from the Overview (four clicks) or publishing a policy edit (five). This chapter shows the layout, how ForgeCentral reports what the Forge engine said, and how to work safely when something goes wrong.

The ForgeCentral layout

  1. Navigation rail. The nine destinations: Overview, Virtual Trust Zones, Users, Objects, Policies, SOC Ops, Reports, Logs and Settings. The active destination is highlighted.
  2. Page title. The name of the destination you are on.
  3. Live indicator. Live while the Overview is receiving fresh data, otherwise Not live. Logs has its own Live / Paused / Reconnecting badge.
  4. Account menu. Your email address (or identity-provider subject) and your engine access tier: User, Developer, Admin or SecurityAudit. Open it to sign out.
  5. Tabs. Most destinations group their content into tabs.
  6. Actions. Every control that changes something acts on Forge itself; ForgeCentral keeps no copy. Most actions with a lasting effect ask you to confirm first; generating a SOC verdict, saving a plan edit, exporting the log and saving an identity-provider connector do not.
  7. Entity drawer. Opens on the right when you select a user, an AI agent or an object, without leaving the page.

Sign in and sign out

You sign in with your organization's identity provider. ForgeCentral maps your identity to a ForgeCentral role (global administrator, tenant administrator or tenant user) and an engine access tier; the account menu shows the tier.

A ForgeCentral session lasts one hour; after that, sign in again. Sessions are held in memory, so any restart of the ForgeCentral service, including a reinstall, ends every operator's session.

Procedure
  1. To sign out, choose the account menu in the top-right corner.
  2. Choose Sign out. ForgeCentral returns to the sign-in page.

How ForgeCentral shows what the engine said

ForgeCentral never invents a value. Every page shows one of these states, and each tells you something specific. The Overview also has a stale state (Reconnecting to the live graph, keeping the last graph), and a path that matches no page reads That destination does not exist.

StateWhat it meansWhat to do
LoadingForgeCentral is waiting for the Forge engine.Wait; the page updates on its own.
DataThe values are the engine's own, as of the moment they were read.Act on them.
EmptyThe engine answered and there is nothing to show (for example, no zones yet). The page says what would appear here.Create the first item, if the page offers it.
RefusedThe engine declined the request for your access tier, for example Admin or SecurityAudit tier required on Settings.Ask a global administrator.
ErrorForgeCentral could not complete the request.Choose Retry on a page that failed to load. For a change that failed, see below.

Confirmations and receipts

A change with a lasting effect opens a confirmation dialog that states what will happen. Cancel is selected when the dialog opens, and Esc cancels, so a stray keystroke never confirms. After you confirm, ForgeCentral shows the engine's answer as a receipt: what was committed (and at which version), or why it was refused. A refusal changes nothing.

If a change fails with a connection error or a timeout (rather than a refusal), the Forge engine may already have applied it. Reload the page and check before you submit it again; submitting twice can create a duplicate note, a second incident action, or a conflict on a policy that was in fact created.

How current the data is

  • The Overview reads the engine every two seconds while it is open.
  • Other pages read the engine when you open them, again when you return after a few seconds, and immediately after a change you make there.
  • Logs reads the engine every two seconds while it is not paused.
  • Settings is never cached: every tab reads the engine each time you open it, except the Console roles on RBAC (ForgeCentral's own settings) and ReadMe (this guide).
