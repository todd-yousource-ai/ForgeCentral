Policies

A policy says who may do what inside one Virtual Trust Zone: its subjects and targets are objects, and one action applies to every pairing. Policies are versioned. A published version is never changed: editing a published policy mints a new draft (editing a draft replaces it), and publishing makes that version the policy's newest published version. What a policy says

  • Zone. A policy belongs to exactly one zone. The Policies list groups policies by zone.
  • Subjects and targets. Both are objects (). The engine accepts only some kinds in each role: User, Group and Agent objects cannot be targets, and URI, Network, Registry Key and Data Store objects cannot be subjects. The form lists every object in both, so a wrong pick is refused when you save. Every subject paired with every target becomes one rule. When you save, each rule keeps its own copy of the objects' selectors.
  • Action. One of Permit, Monitor, Quarantine or Deny, in increasing order of restriction, applied to every rule.
  • Network match. Protocols (TCP, UDP, HTTPS, SSH) and ports; empty means any.
  • Restrictions. Days of the week and hours, a geography allowlist, and tags for reporting.
  • Advanced. Applied To (endpoint names), Max Classification (the most sensitive data the policy may cover) and a description.

Versions

Restrictions

  • You need at least one zone and one object before you can author a policy.
  • A policy name is unique within its zone, at most 128 bytes, and cannot be changed later. A description is at most 1,024 bytes (text outside ASCII uses more than one byte per character).
  • Ports must not repeat or overlap (80, 80 and 80, 70-90 are refused), and an hour window needs both From and To, with From before To on the same day.
  • Ports are single ports from 1 to 65535 or ranges such as 8080-8090, separated by commas.
  • Maximum classification can never widen from one published version to the next.
  • Every subject-target pairing is a separate rule, and a request has a size limit: a policy with many pairings can be refused. Split it into several policies.

Enforcement is off, and an endpoint that receives a distributed policy acts only on whether ordinary internet access is allowed. Rules, protocols and ports are carried but not yet applied on the host, and schedules and geography are not carried at all ().

A policy cannot move to another zone: changing Zone on an existing policy is refused with A policy with that name already exists in the zone, or the version conflicts. Create the policy in the new zone, then delete the old one. The same message appears when an edit raises Max Classification above the published version.

Editing rebuilds the rules from the current object catalog: a rule whose object no longer exists, or whose selector changed, is dropped without warning, and a policy whose rules had different actions takes the first rule's action. Check Subjects, Targets and Action before you save. Editing also clears the policy's active window.

Save & Publish saves a draft, then publishes it. If publishing fails, the draft exists but the list does not refresh: reload, then publish the draft with Edit and Save & Publish rather than creating it again. A failed Delete shows no message.

Create a policy

Procedure
  1. Choose Policies, then + Create Policy.
  2. Enter a Policy Name and choose the Zone.
  3. In Subjects (Who) and Targets (What), select one or more objects (hold Ctrl or Cmd to select several).
  4. Optionally, choose Protocol chips and enter Ports.
  5. Choose the Action and the Logging Level (Full, Sampled or Off).
  6. Optionally, expand Restrictions (Optional): choose Days, From (hour) and To (hour), and enter a Geo allowlist and Tags, each comma-separated.
  7. Optionally, expand Advanced Settings: enter Applied To endpoint names, choose Max Classification (default internal), and add a Description.
  8. Choose Save as Draft, or Save & Publish and confirm Publish this policy? with Publish.

Both save buttons stay disabled until the policy has a name, a zone, at least one subject, at least one target, and valid ports.

Edit and publish a new version

Procedure
  1. Expand the policy's zone (the list names zones by id) and choose Edit on its row.
  2. Change the fields you need. Keep the Zone as it is.
  3. Choose Save as Draft to keep the published version in force while you review, or Save & Publish to publish the new version.
  4. If ForgeCentral reports Published as a breaking change, the new version may take away access the previous one granted, and it gets a new major version number. The check is cautious: adding a Quarantine or Deny pairing, or narrowing the maximum classification, is also flagged. Check that the change is intended.
  5. Distribute the zone again so endpoints receive the new version ().

Delete a policy

Procedure
  1. Expand the policy's zone and choose Delete on its row, then confirm with Delete. Every version is removed from the list; the history remains.
  2. Distribute the zone again: endpoints keep the last bundle they received until you do.

Verify

  • The policy's row shows its version and whether it is a draft or published; the Restrictions column summarizes its schedule (scheduled), geography (geo(n)), tags and any end date (expires).
  • Search policies... matches names, scopes, protocols, ports and tags. For a policy with more than one rule the scope reads N sources -> M targets, so its object names are not searchable.
