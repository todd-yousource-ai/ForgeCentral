Settings: SOC Response and SIEM Write-Back

Settings>SOC holds the two settings that shape how Forge responds to an incident: the response tiers, which decide how much of a response the engine may propose, and SIEM write-back, which sends each incident's enrichment to your security information and event management (SIEM) system.

How the response tiers work

When the Forge engine promotes a detection to an incident, it can attach a calibrated probability that the incident is real, from 0 to 1000 (thousandths). Two thresholds divide that range into three tiers, and the tier decides what the engine may put in front of an operator:

  • Below p_low: noise. The engine proposes nothing and keeps the incident for calibration.
  • From p_low up to p_high: investigate. The engine may propose steps that enrich and report, and withholds every step that would contain.
  • At or above p_high: propose. The engine proposes the full response, containment included. Approving it is always a person's decision.
  • No probability: until your node holds a calibration fitted from real dispositions, incidents carry no probability and are treated as the investigate tier.

Both thresholds ship at the fail-closed extreme, p_low = 0 and p_high = 1000: nothing is noise, and nothing licenses containment. A threshold pair that is crossed or out of range is treated as that extreme rather than used.

A containment step is recorded as refused even after approval, because enforcement is off (). The tiers already govern what an operator is asked to approve.

How SIEM write-back works

When enabled, the Forge engine writes each incident's enrichment row to one SIEM, with a link back to the incident in ForgeCentral. Incidents above the classification ceiling you set are not written. The engine re-reads the setting for every write, so a change applies to the next write.

The credential the engine presents to the SIEM is not configured here. It lives in the engine's boot configuration on the engine host, so the secret never passes through ForgeCentral.

Nothing is written until the SIEM credential reference is set in the engine's boot configuration, which the Forge installer does not do; your YouSource.ai representative sets it on the engine host.

Microsoft Sentinel and Google Chronicle expect a short-lived token. Today the credential must hold a valid token, refreshed by hand before it expires. A write with an expired token is recorded on the incident's trail as failed. Splunk, Elastic and QRadar use the long-lived token the product issues and need no refresh.

Restrictions

  • Both thresholds are whole numbers from 0 to 1000, and p_low must not exceed p_high.
  • Enabling write-back requires a host, a stream in the vendor's shape (for Sentinel and Chronicle, two parts: a/b) and a case URL base that starts with https://.

Under two-person control (dual control covering tenant-config) this tab shows Dual control and every control is disabled, and neither setting can be proposed anywhere in ForgeCentral. Change them before turning on two-person control, or ask for the change to be proposed on the engine host.

Set the response tiers

Procedure
  1. Choose Settings>SOC.
  2. Under Response tiers, enter p_low (milli) and p_high (milli), each a whole number from 0 to 1000.
  3. Choose Commit tiers.
  4. Read the confirmation (The change applies live.) and choose Commit.
  5. Read the receipt: Committed at version N, or Refused with the engine's reasons.

Leave the thresholds at 0 and 1000 until your node has a calibration. Without one, every incident is in the investigate tier whatever the thresholds say.

Configure SIEM write-back

Before you begin
  • Have the SIEM credential set in the engine's boot configuration.
  • Know the ForgeCentral address your analysts use: the case URL base links each SIEM row back to its incident.
Procedure
  1. Choose Settings>SOC and find SIEM enrichment write-back.
  2. Fill in the fields () and check Enabled.
  3. Choose Commit write-back and confirm.
  4. Read the receipt. Refused with an incomplete-configuration reason means a required field is missing or malformed.
FieldValues
EnabledWrite enrichment rows (checked) or not.
Vendorsplunk, sentinel, qradar, elastic, chronicle.
HostThe SIEM's ingestion host.
StreamWhere rows land: an index, table or stream name. Sentinel and Chronicle take two parts, a/b.
Case URL baseThe https:// address each row links back to.
CeilingThe highest classification an incident may carry and still be written: unclassified, internal, confidential, restricted, secret.

Verify

  • Settings>SOC shows your values; What these settings are repeats each setting's bound and how it applies.
  • On an incident's Audit Trail in SOC Ops, each write-back appears as a SIEM enrichment entry with its outcome; a withheld plan step names the tier that withheld it.
